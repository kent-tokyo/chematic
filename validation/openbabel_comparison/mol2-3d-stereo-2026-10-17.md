# Open Babel 3.2.1: 3D MOL2 molecular identity

## Measured scope

This is a development comparison of 3D MOL2 to SMILES. RDKit 2025.09.3
embeds source SMILES, writes V2000 MOL, and rereads that MOL. Only sources
whose identity survives that read-back continue. Open Babel 3.2.1 writes
MOL2, then **the same MOL2 bytes** are read by CheMatic and Open Babel.
RDKit canonical isomeric SMILES judges graph, charge, isotope, and stereo.
The Open Babel MOL2 writer is shared by both reader paths; this is not an
independent chemistry truth set or a neutral writer comparison.

The selected inputs are the first 160 distinct RDKit identities containing
`@` in `stress-v1.smi`, restricted to 4–20 heavy atoms and H, C, N, O, F,
P, S, Cl, Br, or I. Four did not embed, and 31 changed identity on RDKit's
own V2000 read-back. The remaining 125 inputs and their MOL2 SHA-256 values
were identical for the baseline and candidate runs.

| Reader | Baseline `7d6b092f` | Candidate |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| CheMatic exact identity | 66/125 | 75/125 |
| Open Babel exact identity | 87/125 | 87/125 |
| Open Babel only | 21/125 | 12/125 |

Nine CheMatic rows improved, with zero regressions. The candidate still trails
Open Babel on this selected 3D MOL2 path. The 12 remaining Open Babel-only
rows are retained individually in the candidate report. The separate 2D MOL
and V3000 writer comparisons measure different paths and do not cancel this
gap.

## Corrections

- The 3D tetrahedral perceptor now admits the explicit-H form of two paired
  carbon bridgeheads. The previous special case required an implicit H and
  missed the same geometry after Open Babel emitted explicit H atoms. Three
  source rows gained exact stereo identity.
- The legacy MOL2 reader now treats `am` as a single amide bond, as the
  source-preserving reader already did. Five rows gained identity. Open
  Babel's [3.2.1 MOL2 reader](https://github.com/openbabel/openbabel/blob/openbabel-3-2-1/src/formats/mol2format.cpp#L450-L460)
  also reads `am` as bond order one.
- When two terminal `O.co2` atoms have `ar` bonds to one carbon and their
  formal charges uniquely distinguish O⁻ from neutral O while the third bond
  is single, both CheMatic MOL2 readers project those bonds as single and
  double. Source records still retain the original `ar` tokens. Ambiguous
  charges or atom types do not trigger this projection. One row gained
  identity. Open Babel's
  [3.2.1 reader](https://github.com/openbabel/openbabel/blob/openbabel-3-2-1/src/formats/mol2format.cpp#L491-L525)
  likewise resolves aromatic carboxylate bonds before SMILES output.

The pinned bridgehead MOL2 fixture has SHA-256
`9be082f2bfec599ea07e5d090b4a6298f9874eccf23fa44f63db0f1f46e85759`.
It was generated from `C1C[C@H]2CC[C@@H]1C2` with RDKit seed `20261035`
and Open Babel 3.2.1. In a separate 30-record check, ten new seeds for each
of the three repaired bridgehead structures gave 30/30 exact identity for
both readers; this tests new conformers of the same structures, not new
chemical classes.
An explicit-H adamantane negative fixture (RDKit seed `400`, SHA-256
`03df141f0d42b3f64427405e196985953e6c2d69485c21e8166c5c1d590f74a7`)
remains untagged in both CheMatic readers. The existing 24-case paired MOL2
stereo script also remains at 24/24 exact identity for both CheMatic and
Open Babel, before and after this change.

From the repository root with RDKit 2025.09.3 and Open Babel 3.2.1 installed:

```sh
CARGO_INCREMENTAL=0 CARGO_PROFILE_DEV_DEBUG=0 cargo build --offline -j1 -p chematic-cli --bin chematic
python3 scripts/compare_openbabel_mol2_3d_stress.py \
  --cli target/debug/chematic --report /tmp/mol2-3d-stress.json
python3 scripts/compare_openbabel_mol2_3d_stress.py \
  --cli target/debug/chematic --bridge-holdout --report /tmp/mol2-3d-bridge.json
```

The script accepts an absolute CLI path for comparing separate baseline and
candidate builds. These exposed development measurements do not establish
toolkit-wide superiority over Open Babel.
