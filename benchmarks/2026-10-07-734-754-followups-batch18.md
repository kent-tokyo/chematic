## 2D layout: gem-disubstituted ring atoms

A ring atom with two ring bonds and two substituents put the first
substituent on the ring's outward bisector. In a five-membered ring that left
the second against a ring bond, where it crossed the neighbour's substituent.
This affected phenytoin, 4,4-disubstituted piperidine-2,6-diones and
5,5-dialkyl barbiturates. The two substituents now go 30° either side of the
bisector. A ring that is entered at such an atom is turned 30°: the side whose
new atoms land on fewer placed atoms is kept.

## Speed

`write_mol` on 2,000 exposed-10k rows took 711M instructions with the new
layout steps. The defect atoms for the local fork choice are now found by x
sweeps, with byte-identical blocks on 15,000 rows, which brings it to **679M**
(batch 17: 653M).

**SMARTS `^n`** (2,000 molecules × 10 patterns) costs 294.8M instructions;
the same patterns written with `X`/aromatic terms cost 265.2M. The ~30M
difference is `eval_hybridization` (38.8M inclusive): RDKit's hybridization
model. Matching otherwise costs what v1.0.34's does (v1.0.34 `^n`: 254M, with
its simpler model). Decided.

**Canonical SMILES worst cases.** The audit used the minimum of three runs on
30,030 real audit inputs (synthetic cages excluded): 1.91 s in total, and the
slowest row takes 4.6 ms. That row is an explicit-H 336-atom peptide; RDKit's
`MolToSmiles` takes 1.1 ms on it. The rows that trail RDKit most (3–4 ms
against RDKit's 0.2 ms) are explicit-H aromatic E/Z rows. For them the
complete aromatic E/Z planner writes every marker plan, 288 strings for c5k
row 2984, before validating the smallest one. Caching the writer's effective
bond orders saved nothing, so that change was dropped. Synthetic cages and
tori (48–100 atoms) take 10–16 ms, as in batch 13.

**Octahedral metal complexes** with four pyridines are the largest group
left among the 28 clash rows. Their ligands are spread 60° apart at one bond
length, so the rings touch, and RDKit overlaps them too. Fixing this would
mean drawing the coordination bonds longer; decided.
