## MOL writer speed

`write_mol` on the 1,687 stereo rows of the exposed 10k (parse included):
694.5M → **679.1M** instructions (−2.2%). The bond-mark copy that wedge
placement starts from now copies atoms, bonds and stereo directly instead of
rebuilding the molecule. Output is byte-identical on 30,070 inputs (V2000,
the stereo report and V3000).

For scale: RDKit's `MolToMolBlock` on the same rows takes 1,702 ms (it
computes coordinates; 1,817 ms with an explicit `Compute2DCoords`), chematic's
Python `to_mol_block` 57.7 ms; all 10k rows 3,809 ms and 77 ms. Of chematic's
cost, layout is 40% (SSSR 19%), wedges about 8%, the canonical Kekulé
structure 12.6% (170 molecules with `[nH]` or charged aromatic atoms),
number formatting 9% and parsing 11%.

## Canonical SMILES worst row

Exposed 10k row 4903 (a squaraine bis-imine with two ring E/Z bonds) took
63.5 ms (408M instructions); its explicit-H spelling 104 ms. The E/Z marker
planner re-parsed every candidate string in generation order. It now
serializes all candidates, sorts them, drops any that leave a stereo end
without a directional mark, and validates in that order, stopping at the
first that reads back with the declared geometry, which is the string the
old search returned:

| | Before | Source |
|---|---:|---:|
| row 4903 | 63.5 ms (408M) | **6.3 ms (59M)** |
| row 4903, explicit H | 104 ms | **9.9 ms** |
| 30,000 corpus inputs, canonical incl. parse | 37.42G | **22.56G** (−40%) |

Strings are identical on all 30,305 audit inputs of batch 13
(`canonical_ez_planner_order.rs` pins rows 4903, 4550 and 4111). The next
slowest rows are explicit-H spellings of rows 4111 and 4384 (55–63 ms, 864
and 1,152 leaves of NH2 and E/Z H cells that differ), left as they are.

## CIP: a carbon whose rule 5 depends on a lone-pair centre

In `O=C1C[N@]2CC[C@H]1CC2` (quinuclidinone with a stereo bridgehead N) the
C6 label depends on N's auxiliary descriptor, and chematic gave none. The
resolver now gives a three-neighbour (lone-pair) centre its auxiliary
descriptor in rules 4b/5 the way RDKit reads its spelling, so C6 is `s` as
in RDKit. `scripts/bridgehead_cip.py` on 112 spellings: 3,136/3,136
pseudoasymmetric centres agree (10 molecules, 28 centres) and 4,256/4,256
synthetic bridgeheads.

## 2D layout of bridged and crowded molecules

`scripts/layout_2d_quality_vs_rdkit.py` draws every row of the exposed 10k
and ChEMBL 5k with chematic (`depict_data`) and RDKit (`Compute2DCoords`) and
counts non-bonded pairs closer than 0.4 bonds (clashes) and crossing bonds.
A row is *clean* with neither. Bridged rows have an RDKit bridgehead atom.

| 15,000 rows | Batch 13 | Source | RDKit |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| clean | 10,619 | **14,592** | 14,504 |
| with a clash | 4,174 | **290** | 276 |
| with a crossing | 821 | **316** | 402 |
| bridged rows clean (531) | 48 | **245** | 232 |
| unbridged rows with a clash (14,469) | 3,705 | **78** | 174 |
| unbridged rows with a crossing | 644 | **77** | 112 |

Changes in `chematic-depict`'s layout:

- a chain atom with three or more unplaced neighbours spreads them evenly
  instead of stacking them on the ±30° zigzag (most of the clashes);
- a chain atom is placed in the first free direction 30° steps away from
  its ideal one when another atom is already there;
- a ring that shares a path of three or more atoms with placed rings (the
  second ring of norbornane, tropane, quinuclidine) puts its new atoms on
  the polygon through that path's ends, scaled to fit, on the side with
  fewer placed atoms; when both sides retrace placed atoms
  (bicyclo[2.2.2]) the bridge is bowed across between the bridgeheads;
- a substituent of a ring atom is never pointed at the ring's centre;
- after placement, each clash or crossing is relieved by mirroring an
  acyclic branch across its bond or turning it 30° or 60° about its hinge
  atom (turns only about atoms without a double bond), keeping the move
  that removes most clashes without adding crossings.

A mirror keeps every double bond's drawn geometry, and the MOL writer still
reflects E/Z bonds and places wedges afterwards. RDKit reads back the
written stereo rows as before: 1,686/1,687 and 1,670/1,670 (Kekulé-written
15k 3,356/3,357). Bridged rows still clash more often than RDKit's (212
against 102): mostly fused cages (hexamine-like aminals, benzo-fused
bicyclic amines, polycyclic lactones) whose third ring has no free side.

## SMARTS `^n` and Kekulé reactants: speed

Against v1.0.34 (instructions; parsing excluded):

| | v1.0.34 | Batch 13 | Source |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 2,000 molecules × 10 `^n` patterns | 254M | 356M | **294M** |
| the same with `X`/aromatic patterns instead of `^n` | 261M | 265M | 265M |
| `run_reactants`, 11 cases × 100 (8 Kekulé-written reactants) | 78.5M | 181M | **164M** |
| parsing the 2,000 molecules | 77.1M | 79.6M | 79.6M |

`^n` now follows RDKit's hybridization model (so its matches differ from
v1.0.34's: 35,176 against 34,320 here); every atom's code is now
computed once per molecule and memoized, with H counts, degrees,
conjugation candidates and conjugated bonds shared across the atoms. In
reactions the Kekulé matcher's adjacency is one compressed table instead of
a vector per atom, and the product sanitize check reads Kekulé orders from a
vector. Outputs are identical to batch 13's on all 20,000 match sets and
1,100 product sets. What remains above v1.0.34 in `run_reactants` is the
RDKit aromaticity of Kekulé-written reactants (about 41M) and RDKit's
product sanitize check (about 25M): v1.0.34 matched aromatic templates
against the Kekulé form as written and returned no products for five of
the eight Kekulé-written reactants (the `[c]`/`[n]` templates).
