## Comparator policy (items 2, 3)

`validation/rdkit-comparator-platform-policy-v1.json` names, for each
platform, the one RDKit 2026.3.6 wheel a gate compares against, with its
SHA-256.

| Platform | Wheel | Same-process lanes (autoconf) |
|---|---|---|
| Linux x86-64 | `…manylinux_2_28_x86_64` | yes (published sdist; chematic's Linux wheels are CPython 3.9, RDKit has none) |
| Linux aarch64 | `…manylinux_2_28_aarch64` | yes (no CI runner) |
| macOS arm64 | `…macosx_11_0_arm64` | yes |
| macOS x86-64 | — (PyPI has none, checked 2026-10-08) | not applicable |
| Windows amd64 | `…win_amd64` | yes |

PyPI does have a Windows wheel, so the earlier "no Windows RDKit" note is
corrected.

- **Rule.** A platform with no pinned build runs only chematic's side of
  each gate, and the result is compared on Linux. A lane that needs both
  engines in one process is listed as not applicable there; another RDKit
  version is never used instead.
- **Installer.** `scripts/install_pinned_rdkit.py` installs the platform's
  wheel after checking its digest, and exits 3 where the policy pins none.
- **Workflow.** The published-wheel workflow uses the installer in its
  compare jobs. Its new `autoconf` job runs the pinned xsmarts-autoconf lane
  on Linux (sdist), macOS arm64 and Windows.
- **Test.** `scripts/tests/test_comparator_policy.py` checks that the
  workflow's matrices follow the policy.

The macOS and Windows numbers need the workflow dispatched with
`version=1.0.38`.

## Property-based SMIRKS fuzzing (item 4)

`scripts/smirks_property_fuzz.py` writes rules from the reactants
themselves.

**Generation.** From a molecule it takes a connected set of 1–4 atoms. It
writes a reactant template from true primitives:

- element as `#n` or a symbol;
- `H`, `D`, `X`, ring membership, `a`/`A`, charge, ring size;
- joined by `;`, `&` or implicit AND, sometimes with an OR;
- bond primitives `-`, `=`, `:`, `~`, `@`, `!@`.

About one template in seven gets a primitive its atom fails. The product
template then makes one or two edits: bond order, bond break, charge, H
count, element, atom deletion or a new atom.

**Comparison.** Each template is applied to its source and to three other
reactants of the 400 BioTransformer set, implicit-H and with `AddHs`. It
runs through RDKit `RunReactants` and `run_smirks_checked(...,
rdkit_compat=True)`, classified as the BioTransformer corpus is
(`classify()` is now shared).

**Decided classes.**

- `implicit_h_equivalent`: an explicit-H run where chematic gives exactly
  RDKit's implicit-H products (#734 item 5).
- `same_graph_kekule_order`: the raw products are the same graphs, and only
  RDKit's kekulization differs. RDKit's kekulization depends on atom order:
  `[c+]1ccccc1C` gives `CC1=CC=CC=[C+]1`, while `Cc1[c+]cccc1` gives
  `CC1=[C+]C=CC=C1`.

Every `differ` and `chematic_refused` case is shrunk to a minimal probe.

**Run.** Seed 754, 2,000 templates, 16,000 applications.

|---|---:|---:|
| implicit: exact | 2,379 | **2,482** |
| implicit: differ | 125 | **28** |

### Defects it found, fixed

- **SMARTS lowercase symbols.** A lowercase letter followed by another
  lowercase letter was read as a two-letter element: `[ca]` as calcium,
  `[na]` as sodium, `[cr6]` as chromium. RDKit reads the aromatic one-letter
  atom followed by the next primitive. Only `se`, `as`, `te` and `si` spell
  aromatic two-letter atoms, and `[se]` now requires an aromatic atom. `[cl]`
  is an error, as in RDKit.
  - Before, `[ca:3]`- and `[na:1]`-templates matched nothing where RDKit
    matched.
  - Test: `crates/chematic-smarts/tests/lowercase_symbol_primitives.rs`
    (counts from RDKit's `GetSubstructMatches`).
- **Element change and charge.** RDKit gives a mapped atom whose element
  changes the template atom's charge. `[N:1]>>[S:1]` on a quaternary
  ammonium gives neutral `CS(C)(C)C`; chematic kept +1 and returned
  `[SH+](C)(C)(C)C`.
- **Explicit-H element change beside an aromatic bond.** The core kept its
  reactant H count, so `[#6:1]:[cH1:2]>>[*:1]-[S:2]` on explicit-H
  2-chloropyridine gave `[SH]`, a radical. It now re-derives its hydrogens as
  the implicit form does.
- **Hypervalent ring atoms.** RDKit keeps a rewritten ring atom's aromatic
  flag (`[c:1]>>[S:1]` leaves the S aromatic). In its kekulization
  (`markDbondCands`), a three-connected aromatic S, Se, Te, P or As takes a
  double bond when its next allowed valence is one more than its bond
  orders: 2-chloropyridine gives `ClS1=CC=CC=N1`. Chematic's rule never made
  such an atom a candidate, so the product was refused (`product_valence`).
  `rdkit_mark_dbond_cand` ports RDKit's rule for these atoms.
  - Applied to every aromatic atom, the port made 34 products RDKit's own
    sanitize rejects, so other atoms keep chematic's rule.

The Rust tests are `smirks_fuzz_probes_follow_rdkit` in
`crates/chematic-rxn`. On the gates these fixes change nothing (below): no
gate corpus row reaches them.

### Remaining differences (83 probes, tracked)

| Class | Probes | What happens |
|---|---:|---|
| implicit, partial products | 21 | rewritten heteroatoms in fused aromatic systems: chematic misses some of RDKit's sanitizable products (11) or kekulizes a product RDKit cannot (9, e.g. a cumulated `S=C=C`) |
| explicit-H, partial / different products | 38 | RDKit keeps the H atoms on a rewritten atom (`[sH]`, `[SH4]`) where chematic gives the implicit-H products; and `[CH0:1]` has no effect on explicit H atoms in RDKit |
| product_valence refusals | 14 | mostly charged ring N and sp3 ring atoms left in aromatic rings that RDKit still kekulizes |
| `ambiguous_stereo_bond_order` | 10 | chematic's typed refusal where a broken stereo ring leaves an E/Z bond order ambiguous; decided (stereo safety, #734) |

These are RDKit raw-product sanitize behaviours on edits that produce
unusual valence states. They are listed in the lane's probes file. The fuzz
counts are a bounded measurement, not a parity claim.

## Upstream probes (item 5)

`validation/upstream-probes/smirks-probes-v1.json` holds seven portable
probes, each a SMIRKS, a reactant, a hydrogen mode and RDKit 2026.03.6's
sanitized product sets. They cover:

- the lowercase-symbol reading;
- element change (charge, hydrogens, aromatic flag);
- explicit-H element change;
- the atom-order dependence of RDKit's kekulization.

They are offered to xsmarts-autoconf as candidate flags. Contributing them
upstream needs the maintainer, since this session cannot open pull requests
there.

To regenerate RDKit's products for a probe:

```python
from rdkit import Chem
from rdkit.Chem import AllChem

rxn = AllChem.ReactionFromSmarts(probe["smirks"])
mol = Chem.MolFromSmiles(probe["reactant"])
if probe["hydrogens"] == "explicit_h":
    mol = Chem.AddHs(mol)
products = set()
for product_set in rxn.RunReactants((mol,)):
    try:
        smiles = []
        for m in product_set:
            Chem.SanitizeMol(m)
            smiles.extend(Chem.MolToSmiles(Chem.RemoveHs(m)).split("."))
    except Exception:
        continue  # an unsanitizable product set is not a product
    products.add(".".join(sorted(smiles)))
```

## The eight differences (item 6)

Each entry of `validation/xsmarts_autoconf_expected.json` now carries
`compatibility_option: false` and its decision.

- `match.ring_count_basis`: RDKit's symmetrized SSSR depends on atom order;
  the SMARTS profiles give RDKit's counts where they do not. Native ring
  counts stay SSSR.
- Four raw-product policies (`colon_product_bond`, `product_h_atom_added`,
  `valence_invalid_raw`, and the implicit-H trio `hcount_query_explicit_h`,
  `product_h_pin`, `star_on_explicit_h`):
  - every RDKit product involved fails RDKit's own `SanitizeMol`;
  - an option returning them would hand out molecules chematic cannot write
    or re-read;
  - the sanitized views agree;
  - `run_smirks_checked` reports the refused match.
- `fragmented_reactant`: an adapter difference. Called as RDKit's adapter
  calls RDKit, chematic gives RDKit's product.

None becomes an option. The lane stays 75/83, with the same 8 differences
and none unexpected, and is reported as bounded, not as parity (item 7).
