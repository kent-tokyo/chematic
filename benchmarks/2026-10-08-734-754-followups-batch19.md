|---|---:|---:|---:|
| clean | 14,722 | **14,757** | 14,504 |
| with a crossing | 277 | **242** | 402 |
| with a clash | 28 | **25** | 276 |
| bond off by > 20% | 276 | 278 | 540 |
| narrow branch atoms (rows) | 2,162 (317) | **1,876 (298)** | 0 |
| bridged rows clean (531) | 290 | **324** | 232 |
| bridged rows with more crossings than RDKit | 42 | **31** | — |

44 rows have fewer clashes and crossings than in batch 18. Two have more:

- `Oc1cccc(CCCCCC(F)(F)...)c1O` gains one crossing;
- perfluorotributylamine goes from 0 clashes and 2 crossings to 2 and 13.

The changes:

- **Adamantane-type cages.** A ten-atom ring system with four bridgeheads,
  each pair joined through one atom (adamantane, hexamine, N-alkyl
  hexaminiums), was drawn with one pair of atoms 0.37 bond lengths apart.
  No polygon placement or stress majorization separated them. Such a system
  is now drawn as RDKit's projection. The bridgeheads are assigned so that
  no atom with a substituent lies inside the outline, and an anchored cage
  keeps its entry atom in place. The projection has one long bond and one
  crossing, as RDKit's does. The two hexaminium rows no longer have a clash.
- **Wider turns for small substituents.** The clash relief turned a branch
  by 30° or 60° about its hinge. A one- or two-atom substituent may now turn
  by up to 150°. A tropane's N-methyl and the gem-dimethyl of a pinane bridge
  then go into a free face instead of across a ring bond. RDKit draws these
  without a crossing too. Allowing the wider turns for branches of up to 12
  atoms gave 11 more better rows and 2 worse ones, and narrower forks in 15
  more rows, so the limit stays at two atoms.
- **Forks of 90°.** Where a component takes the narrow ±30° forks, the
  layout also tries ±45° (90° between the two branches). It keeps that
  drawing when it has no more clashes and no more defects.
  - Components of more than 60 atoms are relieved this way only when, before
    relief, the ±45° drawing does as well as the ±30° one.
  - Relieving them always would bring the narrow atoms down to 936. It would
    cost 165M more instructions on 2,000 rows (+25% of `write_mol`), because
    the long peptides' relief is the cost.
  - The remaining narrow forks are in those peptides (1,745 of the 1,876
    atoms are in components of more than 60 atoms). Decided.
- **No stacked atoms from the relief.** Two triterpenoid amides drew an
  amide branch on a ring centre after batch 18. The relief accepted a move
  that lowered the clash count but stacked a moved atom on one that stays.
  Such moves (within a fifth of a bond) are now refused. Refusing them is
  why perfluorotributylamine gets the worse drawing above: its best path
  needs a stacked step that a later move clears. Allowing that step and
  rerunning the relief only when atoms stay stacked gives 43 better rows
  and none worse against batch 18, but costs 47M instructions more on 2,000
  rows (+7%), so it was not kept.
- **Gem-disubstituted ring atoms.** Batch 18's ±30° pair is now an even
  split of the exterior angle: 80°/80°/120°/80° at a cyclohexane carbon.
  The triphenyl pyrazolidinetrione no longer has a crossing. When a ring is
  entered at such an atom, the side choice now also counts the second
  substituent's spot.

The new cases in `bridged_and_crowded_layouts_have_no_clash_or_crossing`
(the cages; tropanes and a pinanol, now required to draw without a crossing)
and `crowded_forks_open_to_90_degrees_when_that_does_as_well` fail on the
batch-18 layout.

**Fullerene adducts** (3 rows) keep 2–8 near pairs and 24–27 crossings
inside the cage. That is a sphere drawn flat. RDKit draws them with 1 near
pair, 35–38 crossings and bonds up to 14 times too long. Decided.

## Speed

All of the following give identical output: MOL blocks for the output-only
changes, SVG text and canonical strings.

| | Batch 18 | Source |
|---|---:|---:|
| `write_mol`, 2,000 exposed-10k rows (parse included) | 679M | **660M** |
| `depict_svg`, the same 2,000 rows (parse and layout included) | 1,374M | **1,052M** |
| canonical SMILES, 30,305 audit inputs (parse included) | 20.70G | **19.34G** |

- **Layout.** Each component's bond list is built once and shared by every
  defect count. Bond-crossing tests check bounding boxes first, and stress
  majorization computes its pair weights once per run. Together these paid
  for this batch's new layout steps. The ±45° trial costs about 2M, and the
  wider turns about 12M.
- **SVG.** Coordinates were formatted by the general float formatter
  (`{:.2}`), most of an SVG's cost after layout. A fixed two-decimal writer
  now gives the same text: it rounds half to even on the exact binary value,
  as the formatter does, and falls back to the formatter near a tie, for
  large values and for non-finite ones. A test checks it against the
  formatter on 400,000 random values and on every eighth with its
  neighbours. Bond lines are written without intermediate strings.
- **Canonical SMILES.** The pass that tries other E/Z marker spellings
  returns before ranking the atoms when the molecule has no stereo double
  bond. Strings are identical on all 30,305 audit inputs.
- **Slowest canonical rows (analysed, unchanged).** The 333-atom
  explicit-H peptide (x10k row 1144, 26.7M instructions) spends half its
  time refining ranks and 30% finding orbit representatives. ChEMBL-5k
  row 2984 (24.5M) spends 87% serializing the aromatic E/Z planner's
  candidate strings. Two attempts were slower or gained nothing and were
  dropped:
  - a CSR adjacency in `refine_ranks` (+8% on the audit);
  - caching effective bond orders.
